RepiqueCode

Hands in the air draw, dance and play. No sensors, no controllers: only a camera and the device. A work that dialogues with Uruguayan candombe.

01 — The work

Repique Code is an instrument-installation that runs in the web browser and is played with the body. A camera reads the visitor's hands and arms in real time, and three modes turn those gestures into image and sound.

Drawing. With a gesture you draw in the air, and the lines appear on screen, shaken by the candombe rhythm.
Dance. With the arms you dance: thirteen movement gestures become sound and image.
Music. With the hands you play chords and notes over candombe-based rhythms. Hand height sets the tempo, the opening shapes the filter, and a looper records what you play.

No one needs to know music, drawing or dance. Two people can draw together or play together. At the end, each visitor can download the image they created and take it home.

The work is inspired by and dialogues with Uruguayan candombe, inscribed by UNESCO in 2009 as Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. It includes a real recording of candombe drums from an open dataset.

02 — The story

How it came to be

One night, away from the city, I looked up and saw that the sky had changed: new lights crossing it, satellites, many of them. I thought they looked like people passing through other people's lives. They appear, draw a line, touch other points and move on. I wanted some control over that change, to point at the sky and launch them myself. That idea became Vínculos.

To build it I had to learn hand tracking with a camera, sound and physics running inside the browser. Every technology I found opened a new door. The hands that were learning to launch satellites were the same hands that knew candombe, the drum tradition of my country.

Repique Code is what that search left behind: a repository of possibilities that became a work in itself.

Some decisions shaped it. I chose the camera as the only sensor, so anyone with a laptop can play. The image never leaves the device. Drawing comes first, so that people who do not play music can enter. And the code is open: the work keeps changing with the ideas of the people who try it.

I spent fifteen years as a lawyer defending common spaces. I came to art through artificial intelligence. I believe the best way to go through this technological wave is to take the same technology and give it direction, as I wanted to do with those satellites.

04 — The artist

Damián Lafferranderie Peña (Maldonado, Uruguay, 1983) is a Uruguayan artist and creator based in Barcelona since 2023. He works at the crossroads of body, technology and cultural heritage. His interactive works run in the web browser and use the camera as their only sensor: the visitor's hands become the instrument.

Before his artistic practice he spent fifteen years as a lawyer and university lecturer in Uruguay, defending the coast and common spaces. He holds a Doctorate in Law and postgraduate training in Integrated Coastal Management. Artificial intelligence brought him to art.

He directs Estudioprompt, an artificial intelligence studio, and leads AI training programmes for the Spanish Chambers of Commerce, the civic centres of Barcelona and several universities.
